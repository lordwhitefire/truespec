# Seed Content — TrueSpec Automotive demo

**For:** the developer building the prototype. Copy from this into a seed script.
**Purpose:** 15 vehicles, all three tabs populated, enough for the demo to look finished rather than half-built.

---

## How this is written

Deliberate choices, because they are what separate a believable inventory from a placeholder:

- **Every car is a Toyota or Honda except two.** That is what actually moves in this market. A demo with a Ferrari, a Mercedes *and* a Benz would signal nobody knows the business.
- **Mileage is never round.** 61,204 not 60,000. Round numbers read as invented.
- **Prices are Naira, ending in 0,000.** That is how local prices are actually quoted. They are not "computer numbers."
- **Features are written the way a seller types**, not the way a brochure does — short, practical, occasionally inconsistent.
- **Two cars are On Order with real arrival dates**, and one Landed. Those are what make the tabs look alive.
- **Costs are plausible and produce real margins.** When the client clicks into the admin and sees the profit column, the numbers have to hold up or the whole thing looks invented.

**FX rate used throughout: 1,000 NGN = 1 USD.** Set in Settings; changeable.

## Sourcing the photos

**Unsplash will not have "2018 Toyota Camry in Lagos."** Three rules make it work anyway:

1. **Only use photos where the badge is not readable.** A beautiful generic three-quarter shot of a white sedan under `Toyota Camry XLE` beats a recognisable car that is obviously the wrong model. The copy carries the identity; the photo carries the feeling.
2. **Search per car, curate by hand.** `"white sedan exterior"` for the Camry, `"silver suv road"` for the Highlander, `"black truck"` for the Tacoma. Expect to keep 3–4 from every 30 results.
3. **Get colour to match the listing.** The copy says Pearl White — the photo must be a white car. Mismatched colour is the giveaway buyers notice fastest.

**Usable search terms:**

| Car | Search |
|---|---|
| Land Cruiser / Prado | `white suv offroad`, `large suv desert road`, `suv mountain` |
| Camry / Corolla / Accord | `white sedan exterior`, `black sedan parked`, `silver sedan road` |
| Highlander / RAV4 / Venza / CR-V | `silver suv exterior`, `grey suv city`, `suv family` |
| Tacoma / Hilux | `pickup truck exterior`, `black truck road` |
| Lexus RX | `grey luxury suv`, `luxury car night` |
| Sienna | `minivan silver`, `large mpv` |

**Do not** use recognisable Tesla, BMW, or Ferrari photos anywhere — they will contradict the listing on sight and break the illusion the whole demo depends on.

## Admin copy that should exist

Not per-car, but text the operator sees:

- **Settings:** tagline, WhatsApp number, FX rate, full tank default
- **Summary page:** total inventory, total landed cost, total projected profit
- **Empty states:** each tab needs a designed empty state. *Available* says *"No vehicles available right now — check back soon."* Something a real business would say.
- **Audit line:** *"Updated by [operator] on [timestamp]"*

## The two numbers worth pointing at

When the client opens the summary, he will land on the profit column. Two of these fifteen cars are deliberately awkward:

- **Sienna** — thin margin
- **Hilux** — highest margin and visibly the highest mileage

A demo where every car is profitable by a tidy amount reads as decoration. **The profit column has to be a calculation.** Those two cars are what make it one.
