# Summarize the recommendation

**S** — Summarize. The closing step of CIRCLES. It exists to stop a rambling conclusion, and it is
where a well-reasoned answer is either made usable or lost.

## Why the step exists

Each letter of CIRCLES is written against a specific failure. This one is against **the rambling
conclusion**: an analysis that was good, followed by a summary that leaves the reader to reconstruct
the recommendation themselves.

The method's own framing is a **30-second elevator pitch**: recommend the thing, say what it is and
what it benefits, justify it over the alternatives. Clear, actionable, confident, and backed by
evidence rather than by tone.

## The four things a summary owes

### 1. One recommendation

Not options, not a ranking, not a "the team should consider". **One**, chosen.

A summary that leaves two live options has not decided, and the decision falls to whoever reads it —
which is the outcome this whole method exists to avoid.

### 2. One success metric, specific enough to be a test

The requirement is that the metric can **distinguish "it worked" from "people tried it once."** That
is the whole test of a metric here, and most closing metrics fail it.

- *"engagement will improve"* — cannot distinguish success from a novelty bump.
- *"30-day active rate among claimed-listing owners, against a holdout"* — can.

A good metric names **who**, **what behaviour**, **over what period**, and **what it is compared
to**. A holdout, a baseline, or a target is what makes it a test rather than a hope. Where a change
ships, the metric that matters is the one you fixed in Comprehend — that is what fixing the goal
first bought you.

### 3. What you are trading away

**Every recommendation forecloses something, and the summary must name it.** This is not a caveat
appended out of politeness; it is part of the recommendation, and it is what makes the rest
credible.

The options you rejected are the trade-off, stated plainly: you are choosing the core bet over the
quick win, and here is what that costs.

### 4. The link back to the goal

Connect the recommendation to the business goal established in Comprehend. If it cannot be connected
to that goal, the goal was fixed wrongly or the recommendation drifted — and either way this is where
you find out, which is why the closing step carries the check back to the opening one.

## Also worth including

- **What it is**, and what it benefits — for the customer and for the business. Both, or the
  reader is left inferring the second.
- **Why this over the alternatives**, in one line each. This is the evidence the closing claim rests
  on.
- **Anticipated objections, with answers.** The method expects you to be ready to defend the
  recommendation against the obvious challenges.
- **What you do not know.** Where the needs were inferred rather than reported, or where evidence was
  thin, say so here rather than letting it pass as settled.

## This is a recommendation, not a decision

**The closing word of this method is a recommendation, and that is where its authority ends.** You
hand it back with your reasoning and your sources, and where it goes from there is not yours to
settle. State it as your argued position, not as a settled outcome.

## What this step produces

- **One recommendation**, named plainly
- **One testable success metric**, with its comparison stated
- **The trade-off named** — what is given up
- **The link back to the goal** from Comprehend
- **Open questions and objections**, with honest answers

## What this step is not

- **Not a re-run of the analysis.** Summarize; do not re-argue the whole thing.
- **Not a menu.** One recommendation.
- **Not a decision.** You recommend, and it is handed back.
- **Not the place to introduce new reasoning.** A new argument this late has not been evaluated.

## Standing rules for this step

1. **One recommendation.** Two live options means no decision was made.
2. **The metric must pass its own test** — could it tell "it worked" from "people tried it once"? If
   not, it is not a metric yet.
3. **Name the trade-off.** Every recommendation costs something.
4. **Connect back to the goal.** If you cannot, the analysis drifted.
5. **Recommend; do not decide.** Say what you argue for, and hand it back.
