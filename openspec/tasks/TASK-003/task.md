# TASK-003 — Data model. Layer 3 of 6.

**Written 2026-10-13.** A plan, not instructions: it says what must be true and what must not happen, names the choices that belong to whoever builds it, and leaves the rest open. Its twin is `testing.md` in this folder, written for a session that did not do the building.

**Where the requirements live:** `openspec/changes/truespec-architecture/specs/*` (binding — showroom, inventory, money, media, enquiries, office-access, office-settings, dashboard), `design.md` (D2, D4, D5, D6, D7, D10, Migration Plan), and `tasks.md` §1.3–1.4 and §8 (the data-model work items). The older `product-management/data-model-implementation-plan.md` (#5, 2026-10-08) is this layer's source document and is **read, not trusted**: it is stale on the rate (its D3, §3 and the `cost_line` columns — the owner removed the rate entirely on 2026-10-12) and it predates the screens. It is never edited (owner's scope discipline); where it and a spec disagree, the spec decides.

---

## Why this layer exists

The store was written before the screens existed. Now that the UI layer and the business-logic layer are built, the store and the app agree in most places and disagree in a few that matter:

- **the same reference code names two different cars** — the showroom's TS-1001 is a 2021 Camry; the office's TS-1001 is a 2019 Land Cruiser;
- **the money shape the office stores is not the money shape the rule reads** — one lump total against six lines;
- **two columns and two constraints still enforce a rate that no longer exists**;
- **the registries cannot hold what the owner is asked to choose** — a state's colour has nowhere to live.

None of these are screen bugs. They are the store's shape, so they are settled here, before functionality writes to it and before UX's undo, offline and failure wording are defined against it. This is why the owner's build order puts the data model ahead of both.

## Where the layer stands today (grounded 2026-10-13)

**Already built.** Migrations `0001`–`0007`: `office_user`, `session`, `auth_attempt`, `car_state`, `enquiry_state`, `car`, `cost_line`, `sale`, `car_photo`, `enquiry_pdf`, `enquiry`, `tap`, `activity_event`, `setting`, `office_car`, plus indexes. The ledger `schema_migrations` and `scripts/migrate.ts` apply files in name order. RLS is enabled on every table and documented as *not* the boundary (D2/D5). Registry base rows live in the migration (`0002`) and demo cars in seeds — the split D10 decided.

**What the built code actually reads:**
- **Public:** `lib/public-cars.ts` reads `car` joined to `car_state`, plus `sale` and `car_photo` (aggregated by `position`), and `setting`; tombstones (`deleted_at`) excluded. This is the only public read path.
- **Office:** `lib/office.ts` reads **`office_car`** and `car_state`, and maps rows onto the shared rules through an adapter whose own header calls it "TASK-003 seam". The office has no cost lines; it has one `landed_cost_kobo`.
- **`cost_line` is read by no application code at all.** The seed writes six naira lines per public car; nothing reads them. Costs shown in the office come from `office_car.landed_cost_kobo`.
- **The office resolves a state's section through a label → section map** (`lib/office.ts`, `sectionsByState`), so two states sharing a label would resolve ambiguously.

**Two seeds, two catalogues, colliding refs:**
- `scripts/seed.ts` — 25 cars, `TS-1001…TS-1025`, each with six naira cost lines, photos, sale rows for the sold ones, two enquiries, then `setval` to keep the ref sequence ahead of the data.
- `scripts/seed-office.ts` — 15 **different** cars in `office_car`, also `TS-1001…TS-1015`, one lump cost each, mileage in **miles**.

**The identity and asset stores the design decided are shapes only.** `lib/config.ts` demands well-formed Clerk and Sanity keys; `.env.local` holds placeholders explicitly labelled "NOT real keys … consumed by later tasks". No provider client is installed and no code calls one. `office_user` still carries `password_hash`, and the app's `session` row has no link to a provider session.

**Enquiries, taps, activity and PDFs** exist as tables, are seeded lightly, and are read by no application code — there is no write path yet (that is TASK-004).

**And one thing built earlier must not be forgotten:** `car.ref` is already assigned by the database (default from the `ts_ref_seq` sequence) and deletion is already a tombstone, so "given once, never reused" is partly true in storage today. This layer preserves that, and gives it a single table to be true in.

## What must be true

1. **One car, one identity — and an enquiry is not a car.** A car has exactly one row, in exactly one table; one `TS-####` names exactly one car; every row that joins by ref (tap, activity) resolves to that one car, in the office and in the showroom alike. An enquiry joins to no car: it holds the customer and the car they asked about **in their own words**, which need not be a car we hold, and it keeps its own identity and its own change-check. A second table holding cars, a second meaning for a ref, or a car invented to make an enquiry fit, cannot exist at the end of this layer. (Owner ruling 2026-10-13 — conflict 18.)
2. **The money shape in storage is the money shape the rule reads.** A car's cost is its six lines, all naira; "cost complete" means all six entered; a single lump total is not an entered cost and may not be presented as six. No currency and no rate exist in storage or in the seed — not as a column, a key, a constant or a default.
3. **Nothing is invented to make the data fit.** Where a stored shape cannot express what the rule needs — a lump with no split, a missing colour, an unstated unit — the gap is named and resolved at the source or by the owner's decision. Fabricating the missing detail is not a resolution.
4. **The registry can hold what the settings screen offers.** A state carries a label no other state shares, exactly one section, a new-arrival flag, an order, and a colour. A state with no section, or two states with one name, cannot exist — and a state is refused deletion while a car or enquiry sits in it, by the store itself and not only by the screen.
5. **Identity belongs to the identity provider.** No password material exists in any application table. The application's session row names the provider session it belongs to and carries the dates from which "7 days total" and "12 idle hours" can be judged — the store can answer "is this session still valid" on its own, with no paid feature of any vendor.
6. **Every stored byte has an owner we can name — and the image store is real in this layer.** Sanity is installed and wired **here** (owner ruling 2026-10-13): a real project, a real dataset, its keys in the environment only, and the demo fleet's photos uploaded to it. A photo row then holds a reference to an asset in that store and nothing else — no third-party URL anywhere — so the office can tell the owner exactly which assets are waiting to be cleared (the system clears nothing itself — truth 10); the cover is position 0. **No Sanity Studio is built or deployed** (owner ruling 2026-10-13): the asset store is reached only through the API by the application's own server code, so it cannot grow into a second admin standing beside the office — and there is one deployment fewer. Each uploaded demo photo records where it came from, so the whole set can be swapped when the client supplies his own cars. An uploaded PDF's asset reference and its original filename live in the database, and the reference reaches nothing public and nothing unauthenticated.
7. **Money is exact and integral everywhere.** Every money column is an integer number of kobo. No float, no numeric with a scale above zero, no stored total and no stored profit.
8. **The store can be rebuilt from nothing.** Dropping the database and running the migrations and the seed again reproduces the same schema and the same figures; a second seed run changes nothing; and the migration ledger never claims a migration that did not run.
9. **The store can express the rules later layers run.** A tombstone's age, an enquiry's age (the date it was recorded), a sale's date, an attempt's age and an asset's ownership are all readable from the store as it stands, so the retention and cleanup work of later layers needs no new shape from this one — and nothing in the store invites an automatic deletion of customer data, because the decision is the owner's.
10. **Nothing is deleted on the owner's behalf — ever.** No job, rule, script or seed removes a record, a file or an asset on its own initiative: not for age, not for tidiness, not at any interval. The store's obligation is to make the owner *able to act* — it can always list what is waiting to be cleared (a car the owner deleted, an enquiry whose year is up, an asset or file belonging to a record he deleted, a row pointing at a missing asset) — and every removal happens because he did it. When he does delete, it is real: an enquiry's customer details, its imported PDF and the file behind it go, while the car's tap rows stay and the fact it happened is recorded in `activity`. **The only two exceptions are the machine's own litter, and the owner settled both (2026-10-13):** an asset that no record ever referenced (a half-finished upload) may be cleared by the job, and `auth_attempt` rows past 90 days may be cleared by the job — so the one address the system holds is not kept forever. Neither is a record, and nothing else is exempt.

## Constraints — what must not happen

- **No rule bends to fit the data, and no read point drifts in silence.** Writing the demo fleet will move the office's naira figures — that is expected, and the witness is now that the office and the showroom **agree** and that every figure comes from the one shared rule. A number that moves for any other reason is a data change, named and logged — never a rule bent to match it.
- **Nothing is deleted on the owner's behalf.** No job, script, rule or seed removes a record, a file or an asset on its own initiative — see truth 10 and conflict 17. At no age and for no reason: the system warns, the owner acts. The **only** exceptions are the system's own litter, settled with the owner on 2026-10-13: half-finished uploads that no record ever referenced, and `auth_attempt` rows past 90 days. Neither is a record; nothing belonging to a record is ever exempt.
- **No runtime write flow.** Server actions, CRUD, runtime uploads and imports — anything the *owner* triggers in order to save or change a record — are TASK-004. This layer settles the store and authors its content: migrations, seeds and one-off scripts (the demo fleet, and the demo photo upload to Sanity) run here; the forms and buttons that save what the owner types come next.
- **No invented data *inside the system*.** The line runs between the seed and the app: the demo fleet is content we author, so its numbers may be written freely (owner ruling 2026-10-13); the application must never fill in, guess or spread a value the owner did not enter. No hidden mileage conversion in a display layer, no colour or code conjured to make a shape fit, and no stored record holding something neither the owner typed nor a seed authored.
- **Car records and customer records stay separate.** Nothing joins an enquiry to a car, and nothing fills an enquiry's "what they asked for" from the inventory: what shows on an enquiry is what the owner typed or imported, in the customer's own words. A car we do not hold is never invented to make an enquiry fit.
- **No rate returns**, in any form, including a "temporary" helper or a seed convenience.
- **No secret in the repository.** The Sanity and Clerk details the owner supplies live in the environment; the placeholder keys stay placeholders and nothing real is committed. The environment file is excluded from git and is the owner's to edit — the builder never invents a value to make a run succeed, and never commits the file.
- **The public boundary is untouched.** `lib/serialize.ts` remains the only public path; the leak check must still pass.
- **`product-management/` is never edited.** Stale documents are flagged (`tasks.md` §10.2 lists the known ones; the two that still carry the rate — the business-logic and data-model plans — stay flagged for TASK-007, not patched).
- **The migration ledger must not lie.** An applied migration is not edited and re-declared as applied. The design's Migration Plan says database changes ship forward-only with corrective migrations; if whoever builds this judges that a clean rebuild serves a greenfield store better, that judgement is the owner's to confirm rather than a choice made quietly.
- **No spec, screen or product document is bent to fit the store.** Where they disagree, the requirement decides and the disagreement is written down with its winner.

## The data is demonstration data (owner ruling, 2026-10-13)

The client has given no data and wants a demonstration before committing, so **everything the store holds today is demonstration data that we write ourselves**. It may be made up. This settles two questions the first draft of this plan put to the owner:

- **Which catalogue is the fleet?** Neither of the two existing sets as they stand. There is **one demo fleet**, written once, living once, one code per car — and the showroom and the office show the same cars. The old split was a prototype artefact: two screens, two lists of cars, and no thought given to them ever meeting.
- **Where do the six cost lines come from for the office's lump-costed cars?** They are written with the rest of the demo fleet. Nothing has to be recovered, because nothing real was ever there.

What this changes, and what it does not:

- The demo fleet is **authored content, and it is deliberately shaped to demonstrate the product**: cars across several states, at least one sold (so Sold and real profit have something to show), at least one new arrival, and at least one car whose costs are genuinely incomplete (so "still building" and the money-with-reach wording have something to show). A demo that cannot show a feature is not a demo. The enquiries are dated across a range too, with at least one old enough to be due for the owner's retention decision, so that notice has something to show as well.
- The system's own honesty rule is untouched: **no screen, job or back-fill ever invents a value the owner did not enter.** The seed is allowed to author; the application is not.
- The demo data must be **removable in one move and never mistakable for the client's records**, so that the client's real data arrives into a store that can be emptied of demo rows without anything else being touched. Whether a marker is worth its own column is part of this layer's shape decision; the requirement is that demo and real can be told apart and separated cleanly.
- The office's old acceptance figures — the naira numbers taken from the reference — **stop being the standard**: writing the fleet moves them. What must hold instead is that the office and the showroom **agree**, since agreement is what the demonstration is for. The old figures stay useful only as the record that the office reproduced the reference exactly on the day it was checked.
- **The fleet: 15–18 cars, and rich enough to fill the site (owner, 2026-10-13).** It is written by going through both existing lists — the showroom's 25 and the office's 15 — keeping the better-documented cars, giving each one code, and completing the gaps sensibly. Worth saying plainly: the two lists hold *different cars*, not the same cars listed twice, so this is a pick-and-complete rather than a car-by-car merge. Codes may be re-issued freely (nothing has shipped). The size is not the point; the richness is: every showroom section must look full, every office state must have something in it, and there must be at least one sold car, one new arrival and one car whose costs are genuinely incomplete. The fleet list — each car's code, model, state, price and its job in the demonstration — is shown to the owner before anything is seeded, and the owner may strike any car.

## The owner's answers, recorded

**Every question this layer put to the owner is answered (2026-10-13).** The store's shape follows these, not the other way round.

1. **The data is demonstration data** — see the ruling above. One authored demo fleet, one code per car, shaped to show the features.
2. **Mileage: kilometres.** One unit, stored and labelled once — `car.mileage` in kilometres, and the demo fleet written in kilometres like everything else. The miles figure on `office_car` retires when that table does.
3. **A tap keeps no visitor address.** A tap row holds the page, the car's code and the time — exactly what `specs/showroom` allows and nothing more. The `ip` column goes. If tap-flooding ever becomes real it is answered *outside* the store: one Vercel edge rule (available on the current plan, keyed on the visitor's address or browser fingerprint, counters kept per region and thrown away) or a short-lived counter in the server's memory — neither writes anything down. **The one stored address in the system stays where it does a job:** the sign-in attempt table, feeding the growing delay after wrong passwords (C2).
   - *Checked against practice rather than assumed (2026-10-13):* Vercel's WAF rate limiting counts at the edge before a request reaches the application (per-region counters, address or browser-fingerprint keys, 10s–10min windows, all plans); Cloudflare keeps per-data-centre counters that are never shared or stored; Plausible Analytics counts returning visitors with `hash(daily salt + site + address + browser)` and deletes the salt every 24 hours, storing no address at all. And the limit worth knowing: the UK ICO's guidance says pseudonymised data is **still personal data** — so "store a scrambled address" is not a way out; not storing one is.
4. **Both kinds of state carry a colour.** Car states must, per the inventory spec; customer states keep the picker the UI layer already built. The colour now has somewhere to live on both.
5. **Clerk stays as the identity provider.** No password material in any application table, and the app's session row names the provider session it mirrors.

### Round 1 — the questions the build raised (answered 2026-10-13)

1. **Migrations: forward-only corrective migrations (`0008…`).** The owner left this to the recommendation. An applied migration is never edited and the ledger never lies; the rate's columns leave by a corrective migration, and the rebuild-from-nothing benchmark still passes because the chain reproduces the schema. A clean rebuild was rejected: it means rewriting applied files and dropping the live database, for a tidier-looking history and no gain.
2. **Demo photos live in Sanity, and Sanity is installed in this layer.** The owner supplies the project details and the keys stay in the environment, never in the repository. The demo fleet's photos are uploaded and `car_photo` then holds only Sanity references; the third-party-URL branch in `lib/serialize.ts` is removed. Each photo's origin is recorded, the images are uploaded at a sane web size so the free tier is not consumed, and the "no derivatives" rule stands — the uploaded asset is served as it is. The runtime flows (the owner uploading a car's photos, importing a PDF) remain the functionality layer's.
3. **The fleet is 15–18 cars** — written by picking the better-documented cars out of both lists and completing the gaps, shaped so the site looks full and every feature has something to show. See the ruling above.
4. **Retention reads a date that already exists, and nothing deletes itself (owner, 2026-10-13).** The clock runs from **when the enquiry was recorded** — not last contact — and `enquiry.created_at` already stores that, so the shape needs **no new column at all** and the client keys in nothing extra. The system **never deletes customer data on its own**: at the year mark the office is *notified* and the owner decides; deleting an enquiry then removes the customer's data — the row, its imported PDF and the file behind it — while the car's tap rows stay and the deletion is logged in `activity` (the spec already requires both). No `deleted_at` tombstone is added: an enquiry the owner deletes is gone, not hidden. The one-year figure stays the owner's to confirm, and the demo includes an enquiry old enough to be due so the notice has something to show.

This changes *who reads what* — it is not a save button: nothing in this layer writes a record the owner types. The owner's expectation is met honestly rather than brushed aside: after this layer the store is real and everything the business logic computes reads from it, while the forms that save (create a car, change a cost, mark a car sold, upload a photo, import a PDF) are the functionality layer — the very next one.
6. **Registry shape.** `colour` is added to **both** registries, held as a hex string the store validates; `sort` already exists on both, so nothing is needed there; `car_state.label` **and** `enquiry_state.label` each become unique, because the office resolves a state by its label and two states with one name must be impossible; `car_state.section` stays `NOT NULL` with its existing check; and `enquiry_state` deliberately gains no section — enquiry states are just states (R1).

## Process — how to work it

- **Read the code that reads the store before changing the store:** the public read path, the office read layer and its adapter, both seeds, and `lib/config.ts`. The store follows what the screens actually read, not the older plan where the two differ.
- **Reconcile in this order,** because each step depends on the one before it: the identity of a car first (one table, one ref), then money (six lines, no rate), then the registries' shape, then the stores for images and PDFs, then readiness for retention and cleanup. Each step is verifiable on its own.
- **The image store is part of the store.** Installing Sanity, creating the dataset, uploading the demo fleet's photos and recording where each came from happen in this layer, before anything points at them. The owner supplies the project details; the keys never enter the repository.
- **Keep the store rebuildable at every step.** A drop → migrate → seed run that reproduces the figures the screens show is the cheapest and strongest check this layer has.
- **Where the store and a requirement disagree, name the winner.** Naming a gap for TASK-004, TASK-005 or TASK-006 is a result of this layer, not a failure of it.
- **Leave the witnesses runnable.** The office's figures and the public leak check must still be obtainable when the layer closes; a witness tool pointed at a retired table is re-pointed, not deleted.

## Benchmarks — after this, when…

1. …a ref is read anywhere — a showroom page, an office row, a tap, an enquiry — it names exactly one car, and there is no second table a car could come from.
2. …the office shows a car's cost, the figure is the sum of that car's six naira lines, and it is the same figure the showroom's rule produces for the same car.
3. …the store is inspected for a rate or a currency, nothing is found — no column, no setting key, no seed value, no constraint that mentions one.
4. …a car is stored with two lines of the same kind, or a state with no section, or two states sharing a name, the database refuses the row.
5. …a state is deleted while a car sits in it, the store refuses and the office can say why.
6. …the database is dropped and rebuilt from the migrations and the seed, every screen shows the same numbers as before the rebuild.
7. …the seed is run twice, nothing duplicates and no figure changes.
8. …the owner permanently removes a car he had deleted, its photos, costs and sale go with it, and nothing is left waiting to be cleared — and at no point did any job remove it for him.
9. …the store is asked whether a session is still valid, when a tombstone is old enough, when an enquiry was recorded (so its age is answerable), and when a sale happened, each has somewhere to answer from — while no rule and no job deletes customer data without the owner acting.
10. …every public response is inspected, no cost, no asset reference, no customer detail and no office state label appears — the leak check still passes.

## Conflicts found while grounding (logged; the requirement decides)

1. **The same ref names two different cars.** Showroom `TS-1001` is a 2021 Camry XSE V6 at ₦34,500,000; office `TS-1001` is a 2019 Land Cruiser V8 ZX at ₦96,500,000 — different catalogue, different car, one code. The specs make this worse than a cosmetic split: `car_ref` is the join key taps, enquiries and activity use to find a car, so a buyer's tap on the Camry surfaces in the office against the Land Cruiser. **Winner: the requirement** — one ref, one car. How the two catalogues become one is settled by the demonstration-data ruling above: one authored fleet, one code per car.
2. **The office stores a lump where the rule reads six lines.** `office_car.landed_cost_kobo` is a single total; the adapter expands it onto the six-line shape with the total on the first line and **five zeros** on the rest — and the money rule is explicit that a zero and a missing value are different things. So the office's cars currently look *complete, with five genuine zeros*, when nobody entered anything. **Winner: the requirement** — a lump is not a set of lines; the six lines are written with the demo fleet (the ruling above), never fabricated by a shape that lies about itself.
3. **Two columns and two constraints still enforce a removed rate.** `cost_line.currency`, `cost_line.rate_minor`, `cost_line_usd_needs_rate`, `cost_line_ngn_no_rate`; and the seed still writes `currency`. `specs/money` says no rate field, setting key, column or constant may appear anywhere in the system, and the owner's ruling of 2026-10-12 removed the rate entirely. **Winner: the requirement** — they go.
4. **The password hash contradicts the identity rulings.** `office_user.password_hash NOT NULL` stands against design D4 (Clerk owns credentials; self-managed hashes were explicitly rejected) and against `tasks.md` 6.9 ("no password material stored in application tables"). Missing alongside it: the link from the app's `session` row to the provider session it mirrors. **Winner: the requirement.** **Decided 2026-10-13: Clerk stays**, so the password column and any key material that went with it leave the store, and the session row names the provider session.
5. **The demo photos are third-party URLs sitting in the asset-reference column.** `car_photo.sanity_asset_id` holds either a full Unsplash URL (the seed) or a Sanity asset id, and `lib/serialize.ts` branches on which it is. The design and `specs/media` put images in the decided asset store, and the cleanup rule ("only referenced, existing assets remain") cannot act on a URL we do not own. **Winner: the requirement** — references we own; the demo photo source is part of the seed decision.
6. **Nothing guards "exactly six kinds".** `cost_line` has no uniqueness on `(car_id, kind)`, so two purchase-price lines for one car are legal and the sum the rule computes would count both. **Winner: the requirement** — one of each kind, refused by the store.
7. **A state's label is not guaranteed unique**, yet the office resolves a state's section through a label → section map. Two states with one name make that map ambiguous, and "every state maps to exactly one section" becomes untrue without anything failing loudly. **Winner: the requirement** — one label, one state.
8. **Mileage is stored in two units** — kilometres on `car`, miles on `office_car` — for the one number the showroom displays. **Winner: the owner decides the unit**; the rule is that one unit is stored and labelled. **Decided 2026-10-13: kilometres.**
9. **`tap.ip` holds more than the spec allows a tap to hold.** `specs/showroom`: "record only that the tap happened — the page, the car's reference, and the time". The IP is not a name or a number, but it is more than "only", and the retention rules treat tap rows as personal data on account of it. **Winner: the requirement.** **Decided 2026-10-13: the column goes.** The tap row holds the page, the car's code and the time and nothing about the visitor, so tap rows carry no personal data at all; tap-flooding, if it ever matters, is answered outside the store (an edge rule or an in-memory counter), and the sign-in attempt table keeps the one address in the system that has a job.
10. **Two car-shaped tables carry two lifecycles.** `car` has the tombstone and children that cascade; `office_car` has `sold_at` as its only state marker, no tombstone, and no children. One table means one lifecycle.
11. **The registry is missing the colour the screen offers** — on car states and on enquiry states. This is also where the write half of TASK-002's benchmark 1 ("the owner adds a state and maps it to a section") is waiting. **Decided 2026-10-13: both kinds of state carry a colour** — required for car states by the spec, kept for customer states because the picker is already built and taking it away would withdraw a choice the UI layer made.
12. **Retention was measuring the wrong clock (settled 2026-10-13).** The spec measured an enquiry's retention window from *last contact* — a date the owner would have had to key in by hand, and the store has no such column. **Owner ruling:** the clock runs from **when the enquiry was recorded** (keyed in or imported), and `enquiry.created_at` already stores exactly that — so **no new column is needed and the client types nothing extra**. The owner also ruled that the system **never deletes customer data on its own**: when an enquiry reaches the year, the office *notifies* him and he decides. So `enquiry.deleted_at` is **not** added either — deletion is the owner's action, and it removes the customer's data (the row, its imported PDF and the file behind it) while the car's tap rows stay and the deletion is logged in `activity`, exactly as the spec already requires. The one-year figure and the other retention numbers stay the owner's to confirm (`tasks.md` 10.3). The spec's retention requirement was rewritten to match; a conflict between a spec and the owner is the owner's to settle, and it was.
13. **The void columns can only leave by a decision.** Under the design's forward-only rule they go in a corrective migration; under a clean rebuild they simply never exist. Either is defensible on a greenfield store; drifting into one without saying so is not. Named so the choice is made.
14. **`design.md` D6 uses shorthand in two places.** It says `car.sold` while the storage mechanism is a `sale` row that un-marking deletes — which is what the older plan decided and what the specs describe. It also implies a closed/open flag on enquiry states, while ruling R1 says enquiry states are just states (and migration `0002` already records that: "no is_closed, no open/closed grouping"). Named so that nobody adds a `sold` column and creates a second truth about a sale, or a closed column that contradicts R1. Settled 2026-10-13: neither column is added, and the retention rule no longer needs to tell open from closed because every enquiry is put to the owner at the same age. D6's wording is flagged as stale for TASK-007.
15. **Stale plan text, not a storage defect.** The older plan and design D10 speak of "four car groups"; the spec and migration `0002` seed five states (Available, On the way, At port, Cleared, Landed). The spec wins; the plan text is flagged for TASK-007.
16. **The office's own witness tool reads the table that is going away.** `scripts/reconcile.ts` reconciles over `office_car`, and the office dashboard's page cites it. Named so it is re-pointed rather than lost with the table.
17. **Our own cleanup design deletes on the owner's behalf.** The daily job as specced — the media spec's pruning requirement, `tasks.md` 8.3 and 9.1, design D7/D8 — hard-deletes expired tombstones, prunes unreferenced assets after 24 hours, removes rows pointing at a missing asset, and deletes dead PDFs, all without him. **Winner: the owner's rule (2026-10-13)** — the system warns the client, the client deletes; even at 100 years the action stays his. Those requirements and tasks were rewritten to **report instead of remove**, and the store must be able to list what is waiting to be cleared. **The two exceptions, put back to the owner and settled 2026-10-13:** the system may clear only its own litter — an asset that no record ever referenced (a half-finished upload) and `auth_attempt` rows past 90 days. Everything belonging to a record waits for him, at any age.

18. **Car tracking and customer tracking were fused by a join — and the spec said so.** The store holds `enquiry.car_ref text`, and `specs/inventory`'s "Durable reference codes" requirement said *"enquiries, taps, and activity rows join to the car by this reference"*; this layer leaned on that join as its proof of "one car, one identity". **Winner: the owner's requirement (2026-10-13, design D11).** An enquiry is a customer asking for a car — often a car we do not have, sometimes several at once. A join would have to invent a car row for something we do not sell, or stand empty in exactly the normal case, and it fuses two records the business keeps apart: cars are what we sell, enquiries are who asked. So the enquiry holds the car asked about in the customer's own words, keeps its own identity and its own change-check, and joins to nothing — while taps and activity still join by ref, because a tap is a statement about a car we do have. `specs/inventory`, `specs/enquiries`, `design.md` (D11) and the build list were corrected the same day; the column leaves by migration.
